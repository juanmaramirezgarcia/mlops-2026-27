## 1. Summary and how to use this plan

The new syllabus keeps the spine of last year's course (foundations, pipelines, deployment, monitoring, responsible AI) but changes three things that matter for preparation. First, the format is now fixed at five 90-minute live sessions, five asynchronous forums that each recap the previous live session with three set questions, and two self-paced 60-minute video lessons with knowledge checks. Second, two large blocks of content are new: LLMOps (Session 10) and a structured governance/regulation lesson (Session 12, video). Third, the audience assumption is explicit: no programming or ML background. Last year's material was roughly half tool walkthroughs (GitLab, Docker on GCP, AWS Lambda, Jenkins, Kubeflow, Airflow); this year those become short live demos or disappear.

This plan does four jobs. Section 2 shows what changed and where last year's slides land in the new structure. Sections 4–5 give a content decision for every live session (keep, trim, drop, create), a 90-minute run-of-show and a demo. Section 6 checks that each forum is fully covered by its live session and proposes the discussion themes and facilitation rhythm. Section 7 outlines the two video lessons minute by minute with their quiz design. Section 8 is the production calendar, working backwards from 3 October.

The way we agreed to work: once you sign off on this plan, we build the decks one session at a time, in delivery order, and you approve each before the next one starts.

## 3. Design principles for a non-technical cohort

The cohort is made up of graduates, most without a technical background, in a part-time programme, attending online on Saturdays. Three consequences for the material: attention is finite (a 90-minute online session needs a change of mode every 12–15 minutes), vocabulary must be introduced before it is used, and every abstract concept needs a business anchor they already understand.

### 3.1 Slide conventions

- **One idea per slide, stated as a sentence in the title.** "Models decay because the world changes" rather than "Data drift". The title alone should let a student reconstruct the point when revising for the exam.

- **Anchor first, term second.** Introduce each concept with a scenario from the sectors in your biography (retail recommendations, telco churn, bank credit scoring, manufacturing) and only then name it. Reuse the same three running examples across all sessions: a bank fraud model, a retailer recommendation engine and an internal RAG assistant; the forum questions already use churn, fraud, credit scoring and a RAG assistant, so this aligns with the assessment.

- **Retire code and console screenshots from the main flow.** Where a tool must be seen, show it live for a few minutes with a single "what you just saw" summary slide. Command lines and YAML go to an optional appendix at the end of the deck, clearly marked as not examinable.

- **Diagrams over bullet lists.** The lifecycle diagram (Hapke & Nelson) worked last year as a recurring "you are here" marker; keep it on the section dividers. Add three new recurring diagrams: the Dev/QA/Prod promotion path (S3), the CI/CD/CT loop (S5) and the monitor-alert-retrain loop (S7).

- **Target about 30 slides for 90 minutes (including dividers), 36–42 for a 60-minute video.** Session 1 was first drafted at 34 slides with two breakout tasks and did not fit; the revised version has 31 slides, one breakout and a chat poll, at roughly 2.7 minutes per content slide before the break. Last year's theory decks ran 22–42 slides for the same time; the larger ones carried material that will now be cut or moved.

- **End every deck with three fixed slides:** "Three things to remember", "Your forum this week" (the three syllabus questions verbatim), and "Read before next session".

### 3.2 Language

Explain the mathematics behind a metric only when the student needs it to make a decision. Precision and recall are worth two minutes because the fraud team must choose between them; the Kolmogorov–Smirnov formula is not, "a statistical test that says whether two groups of numbers look alike" is enough. Keep acronyms to a glossary slide per deck and say each one in full the first time.

## 4. Live session blueprint (90 minutes)

All five live sessions follow the same shape so students learn the rhythm and you can reuse timing cues. The syllabus states that all new concepts are introduced live and that the forum recaps them, so the concept blocks must cover every term the forum questions use.

| **Minute** | **Block** | **What happens** |
|---|---|---|
| **0–8** | Open | Welcome, recap of the previous forum (quote one or two good posts), today's outcomes, one opening slide that motivates the session. |
| **8–16** | Concept block 1 | First idea, anchored in a running example. In Session 1 this slot is the course mechanics. |
| **16–35** | Concept block 2 | The core ideas of the session. Contains the live demo (4–8 minutes) and one two-minute chat poll on a debatable statement, which is then continued as a forum theme. |
| **35–46** | Concept block 3 | The material the forum questions depend on most directly. |
| **46–51** | Break | Five minutes. A question stays on screen. |
| **51–58** | Concept block 4 | Short: the last concept, then the activity brief. |
| **58–61** | Activity brief | The task and the case read aloud, facts only. |
| **61–76** | Practical activity | One task, 15 minutes, breakout rooms of 4–5 (the group-project teams). One template per room. Each room produces one artefact. |
| **76–86** | Debrief | Three rooms present in 2 minutes each; four minutes of synthesis connecting answers to the concepts and the forum questions. |
| **86–90** | Close | "Three things to remember", forum questions for the week, reading for next session. |

> *Timing tip: the practical activity is where a non-technical cohort actually learns the vocabulary, because they must use it. If a concept block overruns, cut the demo, not the activity. One breakout task per session: a second task inside the rooms costs two or three minutes of confusion at the switch and halves the debrief.*

## 6. Discussion forums: coverage check and discussion themes

The three questions per forum are fixed in the syllabus, so the preparation work is threefold: confirm the live session gives students everything the questions need (the coverage check), add discussion themes that turn a set of individual submissions into a conversation (participation is 15% of the grade and is assessed on quality of debate), and prepare your facilitation moves for the week. Each forum runs from the Saturday of the live session to the following Friday.

### 6.1 Facilitation rhythm (same for every forum)

| **When**                              | **Move**                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|---------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Saturday (after the live session)** | Post the opening message: the three questions, the artefact expected for each (post, one-page map, diagram), a word limit, and one extra discussion theme to react to. Remind them of the Repeater/Rambler/Distracted rubric. |
| **Tuesday**                           | Nudge: quote two contrasting student answers and ask the group which is right and why. Post the second discussion theme.                                                                                                      |
| **Thursday**                          | Challenge: introduce a twist to the scenario (a regulator, a budget cut, a new data source) and ask how answers change.                                                                                                       |
| **Friday**                            | Synthesis post: the three or four best insights with names, the common misconception, and the bridge to the next live session. This post is also your recap slide for the next session.                                       |

### 6.2 Forum 1 (Session 2, 3–9 October) · Recap of Foundations, Personas & Lifecycle

**Coverage check:** Q1 (three reasons models never reach production, and the persona to address each) needs the "why MLOps" and persona slides: covered. Q2 (RACI-style map for a recommendation project) needs the persona framework and the lifecycle stages; last year's RACI slide sat in Session 9, so add a simple RACI explanation to Session 1. Q3 (training vs operationalising) is covered by the new slide.

**Discussion themes beyond the three questions:**

- "The 3-model company": is MLOps proportionate for an organisation with only three models in production? Push them to define the threshold where it becomes necessary.

- Hand them a real job posting for an "ML Engineer" and one for a "Data Scientist" and ask which persona each really is, and what is missing from both.

- Ask each student to name one product or service they used this week that has a model behind it, and what "degrading" would look like to them as a customer.

**Assets and notes:** Provide a blank RACI template (lifecycle stages as rows, five personas as columns) so submissions are comparable.

### 6.3 Forum 2 (Session 4, 10–16 October) · Recap of Pipelines, Environments & Security

**Coverage check:** Q1 requires the exploratory notebook on campus and the pipeline-steps vocabulary: covered once the notebook is produced. Q2 (Dev/QA/Prod and the laptop-to-production risk) is covered by the new environments slides. Q3 (adversarial or integrity threat, mitigation, lineage control) is covered by the new security block; make sure the lineage slide explicitly says "this is how you would detect it".

**Discussion themes beyond the three questions:**

- "Skip QA to hit the launch date": the CEO asks for the churn model to go live on Friday; QA needs a week. What do you say, and what do you put in writing?

- Feature store or not: a retailer has 40 models sharing 12 customer features. What breaks without a shared feature layer? What breaks with one?

- Whose data is it: the fraud team wants to add a feature derived from customer nationality. Which of the governance questions from the session stop this, and who has the authority to decide?

**Assets and notes:** The messy notebook (with a PDF rendering) and a blank four-box pipeline diagram template. Tell students explicitly that they are not expected to run or write code.

### 6.4 Forum 3 (Session 6, 17–23 October) · Recap of CI/CD & Deployment

**Coverage check:** Q1 (CI/CD flow with code tests vs data tests for fraud) is covered by the new test-pyramid slides and the pipeline stages. Q2 (canary vs blue-green under zero downtime) is covered by the strategy slides plus the new zero-downtime scoring. Q3 (A/B test design with metric and threshold) needs the new A/B slide and a short note on sample size in plain words ("enough transactions that the difference is not luck").

**Discussion themes beyond the three questions:**

- Shadow deployment was not in the syllabus text but is in the session: when is it the right choice for a bank, and why does it cost twice as much?

- The rollback drill: a canary at 10% of traffic shows a 2% fall in fraud recall. Roll back, hold, or widen? Ask for the decision rule, not the decision.

- Serverless for fraud scoring: a model that must answer in 100 ms, 24/7, with unpredictable peaks. Is serverless a good fit? What would you want to know before deciding?

**Assets and notes:** A one-page fraud-model brief (volumes, latency requirement, regulator expectations) shared by all three questions, so diagrams are comparable across students.

### 6.5 Forum 4 (Session 8, 24–30 October) · Recap of Monitoring, XAI & Responsible AI

**Coverage check:** Q1 (monitoring plan for churn with thresholds) is exactly the live activity: covered. Q2 (data drift vs concept drift with examples and different responses) is covered by the new taxonomy slide; make the "response differs" point explicit (data drift may need only a data fix; concept drift needs retraining with new labels). Q3 (fairness risk and explainability requirement in credit scoring, and how to audit each) needs the bias-mitigation and XAI-as-requirement slides; the audit method must be shown concretely (compare error rates by group; sample explanations for rejected applicants).

**Discussion themes beyond the three questions:**

- The cost of a false alarm: a drift alert triggers a retrain that takes two days and a data-science team. How many false alarms a month before the team stops trusting the monitor?

- Ground truth arrives late: for the churn model you only learn who really left 90 days later. What do you monitor in the meantime?

- Explain it to the customer: draft the three-sentence explanation a bank could send to a rejected applicant, using a SHAP-style ranking of factors. Is it honest? Is it useful?

**Assets and notes:** Runs in parallel with Video 1 (Session 9), so point students to the video for the metric definitions and keep the forum on decisions and thresholds.

### 6.6 Forum 5 (Session 11, 31 October–6 November) · Recap of LLMOps & Diagnostics (Capstone)

**Coverage check:** Q1 (RAG operational architecture with a cost/latency trade-off) is the live activity Part A: covered. Q2 (two concrete differences between operating an LLM and a classical model) is covered by the side-by-side lifecycle slide. Q3 (corrective roadmap for one failure case across the four modules) depends on the failure briefs being posted before Session 10 and on the root-cause template.

**Discussion themes beyond the three questions:**

- LLM-as-a-judge: can a model be trusted to grade another model? Ask for one situation where it works and one where it fails.

- The guardrail budget: every guardrail adds latency and cost. Which two guardrails would you keep for an internal HR assistant if you could only afford two?

- Capstone thread: "the one control that would have prevented most of the six cases". Push for a ranked answer and a defence.

**Assets and notes:** This forum is the bridge to the group project defence; encourage teams to use their own project scenario in Q1.
