# Session 10 · Failure briefs (post on campus by Wednesday 28 October)

Six one-page briefs, facts only. Each team is allocated one and must arrive at Session 10 having read it; the team diagnoses it in the breakout (minute 61–76) with the root-cause template, and each student uses it for Forum 5, Q3.

| # | Brief | Kind | Room |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `01_zillow_offers.md` · Zillow Offers (2018–2021) | Classical ML · forecasting | 1 |
| 2 | `02_amazon_recruiting.md` · Amazon's recruiting tool (2014–2017) | Classical ML · classification, bias | 2 |
| 3 | `03_air_canada_chatbot.md` · Air Canada's chatbot (2022–2024) | Generative AI · assistant | 3 |
| 4 | `04_dutch_childcare_benefits.md` · Dutch childcare-benefits algorithm (2013–2021) | Risk scoring · public sector | 4 |
| 5 | `05_epic_sepsis_model.md` · Epic sepsis model (2021) | Classical ML · clinical prediction | 5 |
| 6 | `06_knight_capital.md` · Knight Capital (2012) | Deployment failure (not ML) | 6 |

Every brief has the same structure: what it was · what happened · the numbers · what we know and what we do not · your task · sources. The "what we do not know" paragraph is deliberate: students should mark where they infer.

The root-cause template and the model answers are in `../MLOps_S10_Activity_Brief.md` (sections 3 and 5). The case cards in the deck (slides 32–37) carry the same facts for the debrief.

Facts were checked in September 2026 against the sources listed in each brief (SEC order for Knight; BC CRT decision and ABA summary for Air Canada; JAMA Internal Medicine and Fierce Healthcare for Epic; Reuters/CNBC for Amazon; GeekWire and the AI Incident Database for Zillow; the Dutch parliamentary report, DPA decisions and Amnesty for the Netherlands). Figures such as the Zillow write-down and the Dutch parent count vary slightly by source; the briefs use the most commonly cited values and say "about".
